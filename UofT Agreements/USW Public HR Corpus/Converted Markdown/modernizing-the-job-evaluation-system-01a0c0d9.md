## Message from VP PSEC

---

I have long believed that PSEC has two primary functions: to lead with strategy and to support with creativity. These functions are not contradictory. They enable us to provide evidence-based guidance to our community on matters impacting how we learn, research, and work. They help our community respond or adapt to change.

Our 2025 Report demonstrates strategic leadership and creative support in action. It highlights initiatives to apply our extensive research on Artificial Intelligence in the workplace, and to advance our long-term objective to build clarity and consistency into complaints processes. It shows how we collaborate with our community to develop responsive resources that support emerging needs. It shares innovative work to deepen the University’s emergency preparedness and meet legislative requirements.
A heartfelt thank you to all students, staff, faculty members, and librarians who have worked with us, and who continually impress me with your dedication to this community: those who engaged in consultations for our new Multi-Year Accessibility Plan, an educational resource on addressing antisemitism, and institutional reviews of the *Statement on Prohibited Discrimination and Discriminatory Harassment* and *Policy on Sexual Violence and Sexual Harassment*; those who participated in the Muslim, Arab, and Palestinian Discrimination Working Group and Indigenous Substantiation Committee; and many others. May these collective endeavours enhance the success of our University and our community in 2026.
*— Kelly Hannah-Moffat, Vice-President, People Strategy, Equity & Culture*

## 2025: Our Year in Review

---

### Collaborations

Collaboration has long been a strength of our Division. Last year, we worked with divisions and campuses to support institutional initiatives that will have a lasting impact on our community’s experience of learning and working at U of T.

#### Bill 166 (the Strengthening Accountability and Students Supports Act, 2024) and the Ministerial directives on Anti-Racism and Anti-Hate Legislative Requirements

PSEC worked closely with divisions, Faculties, colleges, and campuses and partnered with the Office of the Vice-President and Provost to collect, organize, and analyze data on [complaints of discrimination and harassment for the inaugural annual report](https://people.utoronto.ca/inclusion/complaints/#annual-report). The report, which meets the legislative requirements of Bill 166 and the Ministerial directives on Anti-Racism and Anti-Hate, shares data on discrimination and harassment complaints related to grounds prohibited by the Ontario *Human Rights Code* received by the University between July 1, 2024 and June 30, 2025.
To support community members in navigating the University’s complaints processes, PSEC collaborated with the Office of the Vice-President and Provost to create the [Complaints & Concerns about Discrimination & Harassment](https://people.utoronto.ca/inclusion/complaints/) webpage. This page provides specific guidance for community members who wish to file a complaint related to one or more of the 17 protected grounds identified in the Ontario *Human Rights Code*.

#### AI Taskforce & Resources

In July 2025, the [AI Task Force released recommendations](https://ai.utoronto.ca/u-of-t-ai-task-force/report/) to help make the University an “AI-ready” institution while respecting core missions and values. PSEC co-chaired the “People Strategy & Administration” working group, responsible for examining the impact of AI on administrative work. Informed by the AI Task Force’s recommendations, the PSEC developed a [suite of resources to support staff](https://people.utoronto.ca/employees/administrative-staff-and-ai/) in becoming “AI-ready.” Resources include an interactive learning module for all U of T staff that introduces the fundamentals of AI use, a Guideline on the Use of Artificial Intelligence by University Staff, and guidance for managers on AI in recruitment.

#### Review of the Statement on Prohibited Discrimination and Discriminatory Harassment

PSEC collaborated with the Vice-President and Provost to support the Review of the *Statement on Prohibited Discrimination and Discriminatory Harassment*. The Review Co-Chairs consulted with community members to assess the *Statement*’s strengths and identify areas for improvement. Their [final report](https://people.utoronto.ca/culture/accountability/statementreview/) provides recommendations on how to enhance the University’s pathways for handling concerns and complaints of discrimination. In response to the recommendations, the University is [creating a new policy](https://www.consultations.utoronto.ca/#consultations-on-principles) in 2026 to address discrimination and harassment under the Ontario *Human Rights Code*.

#### Review of the Policy on Sexual Violence and Sexual Harassment & Revisions

PSEC collaborated with the Office of the Vice-President and Provost to support a comprehensive review of the *[Policy on Sexual Violence and Sexual Harassment](https://governingcouncil.utoronto.ca/secretariat/policies/sexual-violence-and-sexual-harassment-policy-january-1-2026) (Policy)*, which included community consultation and feedback from external legal counsel. In December 2025, Governing Council approved a revised policy that responded to many of the themes and issues raised through consultation and external counsel. Revisions include updates to the Definitions and Statement of Commitments sections, a detailed decision-making pathway for when a student is the respondent, mandatory training for key roles, and an expansion of the *Policy*’s scope to include certain forms of intimate partner violence.

#### Multi-Year Accessibility Plan

PSEC developed our [Multi-Year Accessibility Plan](https://people.utoronto.ca/inclusion/accessibility/multi-year-accessibility-plan/) with input from students, faculty members, librarians, and staff with and without disabilities across the tri-campus. This Plan, which fulfills a requirement of the *Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act*, will serve as a five-year roadmap for advancing accessible and inclusive environments where all members can participate fully.

In 2025, teams across our Division identified opportunities to increase efficiency and worked closely with Divisional HR Offices, Faculties, and departments to create consistent practices on our three campuses. Training and resources enhanced community understanding of policy and legislative frameworks, and fair and equitable collective agreements enshrined institutional commitments to employees.

### Human Resources

---

#### HR Services Optimization

PSEC identified opportunities to optimize digital forms and workflows in the HR Service Centre and completed new HR Information System improvements, including a new program to allow changes (create, edit, delete) to mass timesheet attendance entries. Together, these changes will streamline processes and promote a cohesive, efficient approach to HR service delivery for all employees and people leaders.

#### Modernizing the Job Evaluation System

PSEC completed a comprehensive review of the Professional & Managerial (PM) Job Evaluation System to ensure our job evaluations accurately reflect the complexity and diversity of roles across the University and to identify areas of improvement within processes and workflows. Recommendations from the review emphasize the need to standardize tools and leverage technology to establish a systematic approach to job evaluations and job description development. PSEC is working to implement these recommendations while encouraging more efficient and collaborative processes with Divisional HR Offices and hiring managers.

#### Workplace Investigations Training

PSEC proactively delivered workplace investigations training to Divisional HR staff, creating greater capacity to respond to an increasing number of workplace harassment complaints from employees. In addition to these efforts, a spring training session for HR Professionals supported proactive and restorative approaches to workplace challenges, while a fall 2025 training focused on the key principles of procedural fairness, trauma-informed practice, and objectivity in practical application.

### Labour Relations & UTFA Relations

---

#### 10 Collective Agreements ratified in 2025

PSEC successfully ratified ten collective agreements through collaborative rounds of negotiations and without labour disruptions. The agreements demonstrate the University’s commitment to the personal and professional development of employees, including through various benefits and progressive leave of absence provisions, and enhanced language that promotes an inclusive work environment.

#### University of Toronto Faculty Association: Revisions to Memorandum of Agreement, Articles 7 & 13

The University and the University of Toronto Faculty Association (UTFA) agreed to revisions to Article 7: Grievance Procedure and Article 13: Association Relations following a year-long negotiation period and support from a mediator. Revisions to Article 7 include changes to the grievance process, such as revised timelines to ensure timely notices and decisions; the use of mutually agreed-upon arbitrators; and the appointment of a Chief Arbitrator with extensive experience in post-secondary cases. Article 13 now allows Deans and the University Chief Librarian to designate academic labour relations responsibilities to certain Vice-Deans, Associate Deans, and the Deputy Chief Librarian. Designated individuals will be excluded from UTFA membership while they hold these appointments.

#### Enhancements to Simplify the Child Care Benefit Program for Select Employee Groups

PSEC and CUPE 3902, Unit 1 collaborated with a joint working group to revise the design of the Child Care Benefit for Unit 1 members. As part of these revisions, PSEC assumed responsibility for administering the Benefit. The new plan, which provides an equitable distribution of funds instead of basing funding on expenses incurred, eliminates the need for an application. For CUPE 3902, Unit 1, payments for the Benefit were also increased to twice a year.
The University of Toronto Faculty Association (UTFA) and the University of Toronto reached an agreement in fall 2025 regarding enhancements to the Child Care Benefit program for faculty members and librarians. Key changes include increasing the age of an eligible child to 9 (from 6); using information already available in the HR Information System about dependent child(ren), removing the need for an application process; and allowing two parents to receive the benefit for the same child(ren).

### Safety

---

#### Onboarding Single-Department Faculties and the School of Continuing Studies to the Divisional Support Model

Over the course of 2025, PSEC onboarded leadership teams of the 10 single-department Faculties and the School of Continuing Studies to the Divisional Support Model. This Model enables leadership teams to access support for policy advice, preparedness initiatives, and case management.

#### Tabletop Training Exercises

PSEC began including a tabletop training exercise alongside semesterly UTAlert testing to support the development of crisis response skills among staff across the tri-campus. Administrators can practice response skills in a low-stress setting to better prepare for emergencies. PSEC built on the proposal of the Division of Operations and Real-Estate Partnerships to refine the design and execution of the inaugural Project Helix tabletop training exercise to create a tri-campus learning opportunity.

#### Emergency Manager Network

PSEC continues to lead and strengthen the Canadian Post-Secondary Emergency Manager Network, including expanding its membership in 2025. To further enhance this national collaboration, PSEC also established in-camera incident debrief sessions. These forums provide an opportunity for administrators and tri-campus Campus Safety representatives to review and openly discuss the strengths and opportunities of actual emergency responses nationwide.

### Equity

---

#### Fostering Understanding

PSEC offered a comprehensive range of training opportunities throughout 2025, designed to deepen community members’ understanding of key equity, inclusion, and accessibility issues. Topics included foundational learning on 2SLGBTQ+ and gender concepts and identities, guidance on creating accessible events, an introduction to Universal Design principles, approaches to addressing racial, place of origin, and creed-based discrimination in the workplace, and more.
Additionally, we facilitated opportunities through [Open Doors: Faith & Anti-Racism @ U of T](https://people.utoronto.ca/inclusion/faith-anti-racism/) for community members to visit and engage with spaces that serve as intellectual and community hubs for creed- and race-based communities, increasing understanding of the needs of diverse faith-based/spiritual/religious/ethno-cultural and racialized communities.

#### Addressing Antisemitism at the University of Toronto

PSEC supported the development of a [web-based educational resource on antisemitism](https://people.utoronto.ca/culture/accountability/addressing-antisemitism-at-the-university-of-toronto/), reaffirming the University’s commitment to providing welcoming and inclusive environments for learning, research, and working. Building on the recommendations of the [Report of the University of Toronto Antisemitism Working Group](https://people.utoronto.ca/inclusion/anti-racism-strategic-tables/anti-semitism-working-group/) and informed by community feedback, the resource aims to clarify the implications of the existing legal and policy context specific to our University.

#### Muslim, Arab, and Palestinian Discrimination Working Group (MAP DWG) Consultations

The University of Toronto commissioned a Presidential, Provostial, and Vice-Presidential Working Group in 2024 to review programming, activities, processes, and practices in place at the institution and make recommendations to support the University’s response to experiences of anti-Muslim,\* anti-Arab, and anti-Palestinian discrimination. Throughout 2025, PSEC collaborated with the Office of the Vice-President and Provost to support the comprehensive community consultations of MAP DWG. The Final Report of MAP DWG is anticipated in early 2026.
*\*For the purposes of their report and consultations, Islamophobia and anti-Muslim discrimination is used synonymously.*

#### Reference Group on Multi-Faith Spaces

PSEC launched a [Reference Group on Multi-Faith Spaces](https://people.utoronto.ca/inclusion/faith-anti-racism/) to ensure accessible and inclusive spaces are available across the tri-campus that meet the needs of our religious/spiritual/faith- and creed-based communities.

Read about these initiatives and many others in the [2025 Equity, Diversity & Inclusion Report](https://people.utoronto.ca/about/reports/#edi).

### Indigenous Initiatives

---

#### Indigenous Substantiation Recommendations Report

A new Indigenous Substantiation Framework will be introduced at the University to help ensure that opportunities and material benefit intended for Indigenous Peoples reach the communities they are meant to serve. The framework is outlined in the [Indigenous Substantiation Recommendations Report](https://people.utoronto.ca/news/indigenous-substantiation-report/), which was finalized in 2025 following consultation with the University community and external partners. The University has begun working towards the implementation of the recommendations, including introducing an Indigenous-led substantiation process in partnership with advisors and partners across the tri-campus.

#### National Indigenous Peoples Day to become a Presidential Day

The University consulted with members of the tri-campus community to determine that, as of 2027, a Presidential Day will be allocated whenever National Indigenous Peoples Day (June 21) falls on a weekday, resulting in an institution-wide closure and paid holiday for employees. The new designation will support Indigenous students and employees to fully participate in the ceremonies and celebrations that happen that day with family and other Indigenous community members.

Read about these initiatives and many others in the [2025 Indigenous Initiatives Annual Progress Report](https://indigenous.utoronto.ca/initiatives-protocols/).

## 2026 & Beyond

---

Under the leadership of our new President, Melanie Woodin, PSEC will continue to balance strategy and creativity. We will support divisions and Faculties as they implement return-to-campus plans and conduct tabletop exercises to enhance emergency preparedness and response across our community. We will advance our productive relationship with the University of Toronto Faculty Association and work with our union partners to initiate a new bargaining cycle, beginning with CUPE 3902, Unit 5, United Steel Workers Local 1998, CUPE 3261, and CUPE 3902, Unit 1. We will deepen staff capacity to integrate AI tools into their work in responsible, ethical ways, promoting efficiency at scale and innovative problem-solving. We will facilitate two priorities emerging from the Review of the *Statement on Prohibited Discrimination and Discriminatory Harassment*: creating a new policy and inaugurating an institutional Human Rights Office.

## Who We Are & What We Do

The Division of People Strategy, Equity & Culture is a shared service division that provides core services to the entire University. We are responsible for the overall strategic direction of the University’s human resources, equity, and workplace culture initiatives and support the University in its legal obligations as an employer.
PSEC has 18 areas of expertise within 5 portfolios. Many of our units develop policies, guidelines, and processes and support their consistent application across the University. Other units focus on the delivery of frontline services and supports to divisional HR offices, academic leaders, and administrative staff on all three campuses.

## Acknowledgement of Traditional Land

We wish to acknowledge this land on which the University of Toronto operates. For thousands of years it has been the traditional land of the Huron-Wendat, the Seneca, and the Mississaugas of the Credit. Today, this meeting place is still the home to many Indigenous people from across Turtle Island and we are grateful to have the opportunity to work on this land.
