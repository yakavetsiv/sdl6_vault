1. Properties of C-dot/CNCs

The synthesis of C-dot/CNCs was conducted under reflux conditions at different

temperatures in the range from 100 to 140 °C, (increment 10 °C) and at reaction times varying

from 2 to 6 h. Figure S1 shows PL spectra of C-dot/CNC suspensions dispersed in DMSO. The

PL spectra of CNCs (Fig. S1b and d, dark blue lines) are shown for comparison. The C-

dot/CNCs synthesized at 140 °C for 6 hours showed the highest PL intensity and were used for

the fabrication of the temperature indicator.

2. Fabrication and operation of the temperature indicator

Figure S8 illustrates the fabrication and operation of the C-dot/CNC temperature indicator. The

indicator consisted of two compartments connected with a channel. The jacket of the indicator

was 3D printed using FormLab Form2 Stereolithography 3D printer (FormLabs) and Formlabs

Standard Resin (FormLab). The jacket had the dimensions 50 mm  20 mm  4 mm

(LWH). The compartment for the water/DMSO mixture had a cylindrical shape with

diameter of 10 mm and height of 4 mm. The compartment for C-dot/CNC sensor had a

rectangular shape with dimensions 25 mm  12 mm  4 mm (L  W  H). As the top and the

bottom of the compartments were open, to load the water/DMSO mixture in the corresponding

compartment, a 100 µm-thick polyester transparency is glued to the bottom surface of the

indicator and a 3D printed stopper is used to protect the second C-dot/CNC compartment from

the flow of the solvent. The water/DMSO mixture loaded in the indicator was frozen at the

temperature below the melting point of the mixed solvent, a suspension of C-dot/CNCs was

7

loaded in the second compartment and dried to form a film. The indicator was sealed with a

100 µm-thick polyester transparency.

Figure S8.  Scheme of operation  of the device.  Indicator, consisting of two compartments,  one

with the water/DMSO mixture and the other with a dry C-dot/CNC film, which are connected with

a channel.

Figure S9a displays the scheme of the reader utilizing the Arduino microcontroller. The reader

consists of a commercially available multicolor light-emitting diode (LED) module, a

photoresistor module, an organic light-emitting diode (OLED) display, and a switch for a light

source. The outer casing was 3D printed from Formlabs Standard Resin (FormLab). The reader

dimensions were 70 mm × 35 mm × 70 mm (LWH). The indicator was inserted in the port

in the front panel of the reader. The LED and photodetector modules were mounted in line with

the opening of the C-dot/CNC compartment to detect C-dot/CNC fluorescence in the

transmission mode. The excitation light from LED was cut off using a replaceable bandpass

optical filter (580 nm). The reading of the indicator began by pressing the switch button.

Figure S9b shows the algorithm of the reader performance. Briefly, the microcontroller turns

on the LED and after a delay for 1 sec, collects the signal from the photodetector for 1 sec. After

that, the reader displays a "YES" or "NO" statement on the OLED display, depending on the

measured average light intensity. The decision threshold was determined based on the calibration

8

data. The device was calibrated by measuring light intensity under the following conditions:

(i) no indicator with optical filter (corresponding to the minimum (0%) light intensity), (ii) no

indicator without optical filter (corresponding to the maximum (100%) light intensity). The

reader provides a “NO” statement, if the average light intensity reaching the photodetector is

<40% of the maximum value. Such reading corresponds to the dry C-dot/CNC film and indicates

that no temperature violation took place. If the light intensity is > 40% of the maximum value,

the reader provides a “YES” statement on the OLED display, which implies that temperature

violation is detected. Uninterrupted operation of the photodetector can be maintained with a USB

power supply (5V) or battery (power bank).
