1. Introduction

With the increasing demand for electric vehicles (EV), it has become
very urgent to prepare the batteries with high energy density and high
safety. Unfortunately, the traditional liquid lithium-ion batteries (LIBs)
can’t meet the above-mentioned requirements due to the safety issues
caused by the liquid organic electrolyte, such as leakage, flammability
and explosion. Currently, it is a feasible and effective strategy to solve
the  safety  problems  of  LIBs  by  substituting  liquid  organic  electrolyte
with solid electrolyte. Compared to liquid electrolyte, solid electrolyte
has some unique merits, including no leakage, nonflammability, wide
electrochemical  window  and  excellent  chemical  and  electrochemical
stability. However, solid electrolyte shows a low ionic conductivity and
poor contact with solid electrodes, which results in high internal resis-
tance of the battery. Therefore, the development of solid electrolyte with
high ionic conductivity and good adhesion to electrode is a key step for
the preparation of high-performance solid-state LIBs.

Polymer electrolytes, composed of polymer matrices and Li salts, are

one  commonly-used  solid  electrolyte,  which  display  the  promising
application  prospect  due  to  their  excellent  flexibility,  high  adhesion,
good  processablity  and  on-scale  production.  The  common  polymer
matrices  mainly  include  Polyvinylidene  fluoride  (PVDF)  [1,2],  Poly
(vinylidene  fluoride-hexafluoropropylene)  (PVDF-HFP)  [3,4],  Poly-
acrylonitrile  (PAN)  [5,6],  Polyethylene  oxide  (PEO)  [7,8] and  Poly-
methyl methacrylate (PMMA) [9]. Among these polymer matrices, PEO
polymer is a polyether compound with H-(O-CH2-CH2)n-OH structure,
which  has  been  widely  studied  because  of  its  superior  flexibility  and
good ability to dissolve Li salts. In its molecular structure, oxygen atoms
have  the  strong  ability  to  give  electrons,  which  can  coordinate  with
metal ions to form the weak complexes. Consequently, the migration of
Li  ions  in  the  PEO  electrolyte  can  be  achieved  by  the  movement  of
polymer  chains.  However,  the  molecular  structure  of  PEO  polymer  is
highly ordered at room temperature, leading to high crystallinity and
low  ionic  conductivity.  In  the  crystallization  zone,  the  PEO  chain
segment  dynamics  is  decreased,  which  is  not  beneficial  for  Li-ion
migration.  Conversely,  the  movement  of  PEO  chains  is  promoted  in

In order to meet the requirement of solid-state LIBs, the ionic con-
ductivity of PEO polymer electrolyte need to be further improved. At
present,  some  strategies  have  been  adopted  to  change  the  ordered
structure of PEO polymer aiming at reducing its crystallinity, such as
blending  [10,11],  copolymerization  [12],  cross-linking  [13,14] and
adding inorganic fillers [15,16]. Among these methods, the addition of
inorganic fillers into PEO matrixes is an effective and convenient way for
improving the comprehensive properties of PEO polymer electrolytes.
The functionalities of inorganic fillers can be generally summarized as
follows: decreasing the crystallinity of polymer matrixes, increasing the
number of charge carriers, providing more Li-ion transport channels and
enhancing the mechanical properties of electrolyte, etc. Inorganic fillers,
e.g. Al2O3  [17], TiO2  [18], PbTiO3  [19] and SiO2  [20], have been re-
ported to reduce the polymeric crystallization, resulting in enhancement
of ionic conductivity of PEO electrolytes.

However, these fillers (referred to as the passive filler) have no Li-ion
conduction function, which can’t directly participate into Li-ion trans-
port within the electrolyte. Compared with the passive fillers, the active
fillers have the ability to conduct lithium ions, which can establish the
extra  lithium-ion  transport  channels  within  polymer  electrolytes.  For
this reason, the ceramic materials have been employed as the fillers of
PEO polymer electrolytes, such as Li1+xAlxTi2-x(PO4)3  (LATP) [21,22],
Li1+xAlxGe2-x(PO4)3  (LAGP)  [23],  Li3xLa2/3(cid:0) xTiO3  (LLTO)  [24] and
Li7La3Zr2O12  (LLZO)  [25,26].  These  fillers  exhibit  high  ionic  conduc-
tivity, wide electrochemical stability window and excellent stability. In
the  active  filler-PEO  composite  solid  electrolyte,  the  migration  of
lithium ions can be achieved through PEO polymer, PEO polymer/active
filler interfaces and the active fillers. By adjusting the microstructure of
active filler, the migration of lithium ions along the polymer/filler in-
terfaces  and  inside  the  fillers  can  be  further  accelerated.  One-
dimensional  nanostructured  materials  have  a  higher  length-diameter
ratio as compared with particle-structured ones, which can supply the
continuous Li-ion conducting pathways though the polymer/ filler in-
terfaces and the fillers’ bodies, and thus significantly enhance the ionic
conductivity of PEO polymer electrolyte. K.Q. He et al [27] fabricated

LLTO nanowires by electrospinning method and added them into PEO
matrixes to form the composite electrolyte. The electrolyte filled with 3
wt% LLTO nanofibers shows a remarkable ionic conductivity of 4.01 ×

Monaca et al [28] synthesized Li1.5Al0.5Ge1.5(PO4)3 nanofibers and used
them as the fillers for PEO polymer electrolyte. The composite electro-
lyte  with  10  wt%  LAGP  has  the  highest  ionic  conductivity,  which  is
twice  than  that  of  the  plain  PEO  electrolyte.  The  reason  for  the
improvement in ionic conductivity is that LAGP nanofibers can provide
a long lithium-ion conduction path and reduce the crystallinity of PEO
polymers.  Recently,  Y.N.  Teng  et  al  [29] investigated  the  electro-
chemical properties of In-doped Li7La3Zr2O12 nanofiber-PEO composite
electrolyte. The results show that the addition of In-doped Li7La3Zr2O12
nanofibers doubles the ionic conductivity of the composite solid elec-
trolyte, and the Li-ion transmission number and electrochemical win-
dow  are  increased  to  0.19  and  5.2  V,  respectively.  The  improved
electrochemical  performances  can  be  attributed  to  the  increase  of
lithium-ion conduction pathway inside the nanofiber fillers. As far as we
know, the application of LATP nanofibers in PEO polymer electrolyte
has  not  yet  been  reported.  Compared  with  LLTO  or  LAGP,  LATP  has
excellent  air  stability,  low  sintering  temperature,  low  cost  and  high
electrical conductivity [30–32]. Therefore, it is meaningful to prepare
LATP nanofibers and apply them into PEO electrolyte.

In  this  work,  LATP  nanofibers  have  been  synthesized  by  electro-
spinning technique. The electrospinning method is usually employed to
prepare  the  nanofibers  due  to  the  simple  device,  controllable  process
and  low  cost.  The  as-electrospun  LATP  nanofibers  were  subsequently
incorporated  into  PEO  polymer  matrixes  to  form  PEO-LITFSI-LATP
nanofiber solid composite electrolytes. The SCE exhibits the enhanced
ionic  conductivity,  a  wide  electrochemical  window,  a  high  Li-ion
transference  number  as  well  as  excellent  interfacial  stability  with
lithium metal anode. Moreover, LiFePO4/Li ASSLIBs assembled with the
SCE shows the superior cycling stability.

4. Conclusions

In summary, Nasicon-structured LATP nanofibers were successfully
prepared  via  the  electrospinning  technique,  and  PEO-LITFSI-LATP
nanofiber  SCEs  were  also  synthesized  using  a  simple  solution  casting
method. The addition of LATP nanofibers can reduce the crystallinity of
PEO  matrixes  and  provide  the  continuous  Li-ion  migration  pathways
within the SCE, which contributes to high ionic conductivity. The PEO-
LITFSI-5.0LATP nanofiber SCE shows an enhanced ionic conductivity of

interfacial  stability between  SCE  and  Li metal. The Li/SCE/Li  battery
can be stably charged/discharged for 1500 cycles at a current density of
(cid:0) 2.  The  LiFePO4/Li  ASSLIB  displays  the  excellent  cycling
