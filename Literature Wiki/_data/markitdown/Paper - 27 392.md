Evaluation of Cell Death Caused by CDF (Cyclophosphamide,
Doxorubicin, 5-Fluorouracil) Multi-drug Administration in the Human
Breast Cancer Cell Line MCF-7

Received September 15, 2003; accepted December 19, 2003; published online January 10, 2004

We evaluated the features of cell death induced by CDF (cyclophosphamide [CPA], doxorubicin [DOX], 5-
ﬂuorouracil [5-FU]) multi-drug administration in vitro using the human breast cancer cell line MCF-7. Used in-
dividually, DOX and 5-FU induced 60% cell death in MCF-7 cells, at 5 mmg/ml and 25 mmg/ml, respectively, by the
4th day following drug treatment. CPA was the least cytotoxic of the 3 drugs, causing only 20% cell death, even
at  the  high  concentration  of  500 mmg/ml.  Treating  cells  with  a  mixture  of  all  three  anticancer  drugs  resulted  in
60%  cell  death,  on  the  second  and  third  day  following  drug  treatment.  The  nature  of  the  cytotoxicity  of  CPA,
DOX,  and  5-FU  was  investigated,  because  these  drugs  are  sometimes  used  to  induce  apoptosis.  Biochemical
analysis showed faint DNA fragmentation in the case of DOX or all three drugs, but not for treatment with CPA
or 5-FU. In contrast, the morphological apoptotic feature of a condensed nucleus was observed only for CPA and
5-FU. Flow cytometric data agreed with the morphological results in that the FACS cytogram for DOX and for
all three drugs was different from that for CPA or 5-FU given alone. These observations suggested that the cell
death induced by these anticancer drugs in the human breast cancer cell line MCF-7 is a mixture of apoptotic
and non-apoptotic, but it becomes completely non-apoptotic in the case of multi-drug administration.

Key words

anticancer drug; cyclophosphamide; doxorubicin; 5-ﬂuorouracil; MCF-7; apoptosis

Multi-drug chemotherapy a basic chemotherapeutic proto-
col  for  cancer  that  has  recently  been  widely  used  in  pa-
tients.1) Early  chemotherapeutic  cancer  treatments  generally
used a single chemotherapeutic agent. However, single-agent
protocols  had  a  serious  defect  in  that  patients  whose  cancer
progressed could only be treated by increasing the amount of
the drug. The outcome of this regimen was increased side ef-
fects, such as nausea and erythrophthisis.

To solve this problem, multi-agent chemotherapy2) was es-
tablished a couple of decades ago for most chemotherapeutic
treatments  of  human  cancers.  In  the  case  of  human  breast
cancer,  for  example,  the  CDF  (cyclophosphamide  [CPA],
doxorubicin [DOX], and 5-ﬂuorouracil [5-FU]) combination
chemotherapeutic  protocol  is  often  used.3) DOX,  which  was
originally discovered as a natural anti-tumorigenic product, is
considered  to  be  the  most  effective  drug  for  treating  human
breast  cancer.2) CPA  works  as  an  alkylating  agent  and  5-FU
as an antimetabolite. Although the advantages of using mul-
tiple-chemotherapy regimens have been established experien-
tially  in  the  clinical  ﬁeld,  the  advantages  of  this  approach
have  yet  to  be  well  documented  by  results  obtained  at  the
level of cultured cell lines. For example, little is known about
the  nature  of  the  cell  death  that  occurs  if  multi-agent
chemotherapy rather than the single-agent protocol is used.

Interestingly,  CPA,  DOX,  and  5-FU  have  been  indepen-
dently reported to induce apoptosis in the human breast can-
cer  cell  line  MCF-7.4—6) This  led  us  to  think  that  combined
treatment  with  these  three  drugs  might  cause  a  drastic  in-
crease  in  the  apoptotic  cell  death  of  MCF-7  cells  compared
with  the  effects  of  the  individual  drugs.  On  the  other  hand,
however,  although  these  anticancer  drugs  have  been  used  to
induce apoptosis in studies of programmed cell death, these
studies have presented little conventional evidence of apopto-
sis, such as morphological and biochemical data. Therefore,

the  idea  that  these  agents  induced  apoptosis  might  have  re-
sulted from confusion over the deﬁnitions of apoptosis versus
programmed cell death; that is, researchers may not have ap-
preciated that apoptosis is deﬁned according to speciﬁc mor-
phological features.7)

In  this  study,  we  focused  on  human  breast  cancer,  and,
using  the  human  breast  cancer  cell  line  MCF-7  in  vivo sys-
tem, we assessed the efﬁciency of cell killing by CDF multi-
drug  administration.  This  is  a  model  cell-culture  system  of
the clinically available combination chemotherapy. We espe-
cially  focused  on  the  nature  of  the  cell  death  caused  by  the
CDF multiple-drug administration versus single-drug admin-
istration. The MCF-7 cell line has been widely used in stud-
ies of apoptosis, and we have previously shown that the anal-
gesic  buprenorphine  hydrochloride  (Bph)  induces  apoptosis
in  MCF-7  cells.8,9) Therefore,  our  previous  ﬁnding  provided
us with a positive control for this study.

We present two ﬁndings here: First, the co-administration
of  the  three  CDF  anticancer  drugs  effectively  induced  cell
death in the cell-culture system. Second, our morphological,
biochemical, and ﬂow-cytometric analyses indicated that the
multi-drug  administration  of  CDF  causes  a  non-apoptotic
cell death in the MCF-7 line.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Cell Line, Cell Culture, and Microscopy MCF-7 cells
were  maintained  in  Dulbecco’s  Modiﬁed  Eagle  Medium
(Nissui;  Tokyo,  Japan)  supplemented  with  10%  fetal  bovine

MCF-7 cells were grown under 100% humidity in a 5% CO2
atmosphere  at  37 °C.  The  morphological  changes  were  ob-
served  using  an  Olympus  model  IX-70  inverted  phase-con-
trast microscope equipped with an IX-FLA ﬂuorescence ob-

Administration  of  Anticancer  Drugs The  anticancer
drugs CPA, DOX, and 5-FU were added alone or in combina-
tion  to  logarithmically  growing  MCF-7  cells  at  a  ﬁnal  con-
centration of 500 mg/ml CPA, 5 mg/ml DOX, and 25 mg/ml 5-
FU. The cells were kept in the CO2 incubator until they were
harvested. An analgesic, Bph (in a 40% methanol stock solu-
tion), was added to logarithmically growing MCF-7 cells to a
ﬁnal concentration of 100 mM.

Cell Viability, Morphological Observation of Apoptosis,
and  DNA  Fragmentation  Assay Cell  viability  was  as-
sessed using the alamarBlueTM Assay (Tresk Diagnostic Sys-
tems;  U.S.A.)  according  to  the  manufacturer’s  instructions.
The alamarBlueTM Assay incorporates a ﬂuorometric growth
indicator that responds to metabolic activity. Speciﬁcally, the
system incorporates an oxidation-reduction indicator that ﬂuo-
resces and changes color in response to the chemical reduc-
tion  of  the  culture  medium  resulting  from  cell  growth.  In
brief, about 104 cells/100 ml medium were plated in a 96-well
micro-titer  plate,  and  the  individual  anticancer  drugs  were
added. Next, 10 ml of alamarBlueTM was added and the cells
were  kept  it  in  a  CO2 incubator  for  4 h.  The  colorimetric
assay was conducted by measuring the absorbance at 570 and
600 nm.

Typical  apoptotic  nuclear  condensation  was  used  as  the
morphological  marker  of  apoptosis,  and  was  conducted  as-
sessed by staining the nuclear DNA with Hoechst 33258 dye
(Molecular  Probes;  U.S.A.)  as  described  previously.8) DNA-
ladder  formation,  was  documented  by  electrophoresis  on  a
1.8% agarose gel as described previously.10)

Flow  Cytometric  Analysis  of  Apoptotic  Progression
The  ApoAlertTM Annexin  V  apoptosis  Kit  (CLONTECH;
U.S.A.) is an apoptosis detection kit used in ﬂow cytometry.
MCF-7  cells  were  spun  at  5003g for  5 min  at  4 °C,  then
washed  with  ice-cold  PBS(2)  buffer.  The  pellet  was  resus-
pended in ice-cold binding buffer from the kit. A 5% annexin
V-FITC  solution  and  2.5%  propidium  iodide  (PI)  solution
were added to the cell suspension, which was kept on ice in
the dark. The sample was then analyzed on a ﬂow cytometer
(Coulter, EPICS®XL; U.S.A.).

Anticancer  Drugs To  characterize  the  cell  death  induced
by anticancer drugs in MCF-7 cells, we used FACS analysis
(Fig.  5).  In  a  previous  apoptotic  study,  we  showed  that  Bph
induced apoptosis in MCF-7 cells via a mitochondrial apop-

sis  of  MCF-7  cells  as  a  positive  control  for  apoptotic  cell
death.

In typical apoptotic cytograms, intact FITC(2)/PI(2) cells

any  of  the  dying  MCF-7  cells  did  so  following  Bph  treat-

population  did  not  increase,  even  when  the  incubation  con-
tinued to Day 4. The cell-death cytogram of cells treated with

CPA and 5-FU resembled the Bph cytograms: i.e., the major-
ity of the cell population stayed in the FITC(2)/PI(2) area,
even  on  Day  4.  In  contrast,  when  the  MCF-7  cells  were
treated  with  DOX  or  all  three  drugs,  the  FACS  cytograms
were different from those generated by the CPA- and 5-FU-
treated samples. As early as Day 1 after DOX-administration,
the  cell  population  changed  from  FITC(2)/PI(2)
to
FITC(2)/PI(1),  and  this  population  was  dominant  for  all
four  days  of  the  experiment.  In  the  case  of  the  combination
treatment,  the  FACS  cytograms  were  almost  the  same  as
shown  for  DOX  administration.  Given  the  results  of  the
FACS  analysis,  we  concluded  that  the  cell  death  caused  by
CPA and 5-FU closely resembled that caused by Bph. How-
ever,  DOX  alone  and  the  three  CDF  drugs  together  caused
cell-death that had completely different features.

DISCUSSION

In the last two decades, cancer chemotherapies have been
improved  dramatically,  especially  for  diseases  such  as  acute
childhood  leukemia.  On  the  other  hand,  however,  some  ma-
lignancies of adults, for example, breast, colon, stomach, and
lung cancer are still life-threatening. Nowadays, multi-agent
chemotherapy  for  cancer  patients  is  taken  for  granted.  The
greatest advantage of combination chemotherapy over single-
agent chemotherapy is the reduction in the side effects of the
individual anticancer drugs.

Cancer chemotherapy and the ﬁeld of apoptosis have par-
tially overlapped since the 1980’s,12) because of the expecta-
tion  that  the  study  of  apoptosis-inducing  reagents  and/or
apoptosis-inducing  mechanisms  could  be  applicable  to  can-

drugs  have  been  re-evaluated  in  terms  of  their  ability  to  in-

apoptosis  in  the  rodent  nerve  cell-line,  NG108-15,8,10) and
the human MCF-7 breast cancer cell line.9) MCF-7 cells are
widely  used  in  the  study  of  apoptosis  because  they  lack  the
caspase-3 gene.14) Thus, we initiated the present study using
MCF-7 cells, as a model of human breast cancer. To investi-
gate  the  nature  of  cell  death  in  single-drug  and  multi-drug
administration  in  this  model,  we  chose  three  representative
anticancer drugs CPA, DOX, and 5-FU, which are used clini-
cally in multi-drug chemotherapy.

CPA, DOX, and 5-FU are reported to induce apoptosis in
MCF-7 cells. The dose-dependency of the cytotoxicity of the
individual anticancer drugs was determined from data in the
referenced  reports.4,11,15) The  viability  of  MCF-7  cells  de-
creased  unequally  following  the  administration  of  the  indi-
vidual anticancer drugs. Of the three anticancer drugs, DOX
had the strongest cytotoxicity, even though it was used at the
lowest  concentration.  It  was  interesting  that  the  cytotoxicity
of  CPA  was  the  lowest  of  the  three  drugs,  even  though  its
dosage  was  the  highest  among  them  (Fig.  1).  This  could  be
explained  by  a  slow  rate  of  metabolism  of  CPA  by  cy-
tochrome  p450  (CYP  2B6)  in  the  MCF-7  cells16):  CPA  is  a
pro-drug,  and  the  metabolites  of  CPA,  phosphoramide  mus-
tard and acrolein, are the active forms that induce cell death
in  cancer  cells.  Therefore,  the  slow  metabolism  of  CPA

would  result  in  low  levels  of  the  active  metabolites  and  the
need  for  a  large  amount  of  CPA  to  induce  cell  death  in  the
MCF-7 cells. In fact, Yu et al. examined the enzyme level of
cytochrome  P450  in  various  established  human  tumor  cell
lines  and  reported  that  its  expression  level  in  MCF-7  cells
was not very high.17)

However,  it  is  important  to  understand  whether  there  are
differences  in  the  nature  of  the  cell-death  induced  by  CPA
and its chemically activated forms, when given individually.
A study by Schwartz, et al. addresses this issue.18) They cul-
tured  9L  gliosarcoma  cells  in  the  presence  of  CPA  or  its
chemically  activated  forms,  mafosfamide  and  4-hydroper-
oxyifosfamide,  and  compared  the  form  of  cell  death  caused
by  each  chemical.  They  did  not  observe  any  distinct  differ-
ence in caspase activation or in the morphological/biochemi-
cal  apoptotic  features  among  the  cells  that  were  killed  by
CPA or its derivatives. Even though this experiment was con-
ducted in an in vitro system, the results suggest there may be
no intrinsic difference in the nature of the cell death induced
by CPA or its metabolites in human breast cancer.

Co-treatment with all three drugs showed enhanced lethal-
ity  over  individual  treatment  with  any  of  the  anticancer
drugs. The enhanced lethality was largest on Days 2 and 3 of
the experiment (Fig. 1). Microscopic observation with phase-
contrast optics agreed with the viability data (Fig. 2): On Day
3,  DOX-  and  5-FU-treated  MCF-7  samples  contained  large
numbers of ﬂoating cells, and this result was almost the same
as in samples treated with Bph (the positive control)  on ex-
perimental Day 1. These data show that the administration of

Because  these  anticancer  drugs  were  previously  reported
to trigger apoptosis in MCF-7 cells, we ﬁrst thought that the
enhanced  lethality  from  the  co-administration  of  the  drugs
was simply a result of increased apoptosis, as an additive ef-
fect. It was easy to test this hypothesis by observing the fea-
tures  of  dying  cells  that  are  characteristic  of  apoptotic  cell
death.  Apoptosis  is  deﬁned  by  the  morphological  changes
seen in programmed cell death, which include nuclear chro-
matin  condensation.  A  well-known  biochemical  marker  for
cell death is DNA ladder formation. First, we conducted the
DNA fragmentation assay and took ﬂuorescence micrographs
of  the  results  (Figs.  3,  4).  (The  DNA  fragmentation  assay
shown  in  Fig.  2  is  based  on  Willy’s  method  for  detecting  a
DNA-ladder  in  the  lower  molecular  weight  range.7))  Al-
though  the  DNA  bands  with  high  molecular  weights  were
seen in samples treated with CPA or DOX, only a very faint
smear  of  fragmented  DNA  was  observed  only  in  the  DOX-
treated samples. When samples were co-treated with all three
anticancer drugs, a similar DNA smear was seen, and its in-
tensity was stronger than with DOX treatment alone (Fig. 3).
This  biochemical  test  was  less  deﬁnitive  than  we  wished,
however, because certain apoptotic cell-death without DNA-
ladder formation has recently been reported.8) Therefore, we
also examined the cells morphologically and by FACS.

To our surprise, the morphological observations, which di-
rectly  assayed  nuclear  chromatin  blebbing  by  staining  with
Hoechst 33258 (Fig. 4), did not always correspond to the bio-
chemical data (Fig. 4). There were very few typical apoptotic
cells  among  the  Day  3  samples  treated  with  DOX  or  co-
treated  with  the  CDF  drugs  (Fig.  4,  “DOX;  day  3”  and
“C1D1F;  day  3”).  In  contrast,  typical  condensed  nuclei  of
apoptotic cells were readily detected in samples treated with
CPA or 5-FU, even though there was no DNA-ladder forma-
tion in identically treated samples (Fig. 4).

One reason for the discrepancy between our study and pre-
vious reports could be that a different deﬁnition of “apopto-
sis” was used in the earlier studies. For example, some of the
reports  presented  only  FACS  data  as  evidence  of  apoptosis
induction by the anticancer drugs.4,11) Since apoptosis is clas-
sically  deﬁned  by  morphological  characteristics,  we  could
propose that the cell death caused by CPA and 5-FU is apop-
tosis  without  DNA-fragmentation  and  that  caused  by  DOX
and C1D1F is non-apoptotic, at least by the classical deﬁni-
tion. However, we also conducted a FACS analysis to help us
differentiate  between  apoptotic  and  non-apoptotic  cell  death
caused by these anticancer drugs.

The  FACS  cytograms  were  generated  using  annexin  V-
FITC and PI. Generally, a FITC(1)/PI(2) cytogram is recog-
nized  as  an  early  apoptotic  event.19) We  used  Bph-induced
apoptosis of MCF-7 cells as a positive control for apoptosis,
because we previously identiﬁed the Bph-induced cell death
as  apoptotic  using  morphological  and  biochemical  evi-
dence.9) Interestingly, the Bph-induced apoptosis had a novel
proﬁle  on  the  FACS  cytograms  (Fig.  5).  Both  during  early
apoptosis (within 24 h of treatment, not shown) and until Day
4,  almost  all  the  MCF-7  cells  were  stuck  in  the  FITC(2)/
PI(2)  area.  Most  likely,  this  is  characteristic  of  apoptotic
MCF-7  cells.  The  cytograms  of  cells  treated  with  the  CDF
drugs  individually  or  combined  could  be  divided  into  two

Another  reason  for  the  discrepancy  between  our  results
and  the  previous  reports  is  that  different  strains  of  MCF-7
cells  appear  to  have  different  apoptotic  features.  Recently,
Gooch et al. reported strain-speciﬁc differences in the forma-
tion of apoptotic DNA ladders in MCF-7 cells.20) According
to  these  authors,  because  MCF-7  cells  were  the  ﬁrst  human
breast cancer cells to be permanently maintained in cell cul-
ture, “laboratory strains” of MCF-7 cells have developed that
are slightly different from one another. They reported that the
authentic  MCF-7-ATCC  strain  showed  no  DNA  fragmenta-
tion under conditions of serum starvation or DOX treatment.
Our  MCF-7  line  was  purchased  from  Dai-Nippon  Pharma-
ceutical  Co.  Ltd.  (Osaka,  Japan)  and  originated  from  the
ATCC strain, according to the distributor’s documents.

Besides  the  ﬁnding  that  the  CDF  anticancer  drugs  effec-
tively induced cell death in the cell-culture system, the data
summarized in Table 1 permit two other interesting outcomes
to be extracted. (1) The cytotoxicity caused by the individual
anticancer  drugs  did  not  completely  meet  the  deﬁnition  of
apoptosis,  given  that  the  three  apoptotic  features  tested  did
not  coincide.  (2)  Treatment  with  multiple  anticancer  drugs
could  lead  to  a  different  kind  of  cell  death  than  treatment
with  the  individual  drugs.  In  Fig.  4,  the  individual  adminis-
tration  of  CPA  or  5-FU  induced  a  morphological  apoptotic
feature,  nuclear  blebbing,  but  this  feature  was  completely
abolished in the samples co-treated with all three drugs (Fig.
4).  Similarly,  the  FACS  cytograms  generated  by  treatment
with  5-FU  or  CPA  were  different  from  those  generated  by
cells  that  were  co-treated  with  all  three  drugs  (Fig.  5).  Al-
though the same phenomenon was reported previously,21) we
cannot explain this effect, however some sort of interference
in the apoptotic pathway must have occurred.

Diverse  death-signal  transduction  pathways  have  been
documented, and the application of this knowledge to cancer
chemotherapy  is  likely  to  prove  valuable  to  developing  new
clinical chemotherapies. This report is a useful step in the re-
evaluation  of  combined  chemotherapies,  which  have  been
used routinely to treat clinically common tumors.
