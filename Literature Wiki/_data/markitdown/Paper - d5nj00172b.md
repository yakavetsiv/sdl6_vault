Eﬀect of Sb3+ doping on the electrochemical
performance of a LATP solid electrolyte

Jiale Yuan,

Yueming Li,

* Yi Sun,* Binxuan Jiang and Kai Li

NASICON-type solid electrolyte Li1.3Al0.3Ti1.7P3O12 (LATP) is attractive because of its cheap raw materials,
excellent air stability, and high ionic conductivity. However, due to the poor sintering performance and
the easy reduction of Ti4+ by lithium metal, the grain boundary resistance is high and the interface
between the solid electrolyte and lithium anode is unstable. In this paper, the LATP solid electrolyte was
synthesized by doping Sb3+ with a larger ion radius and stronger electronegativity instead of Ti4+ by
a solid-state method. The eﬀects of diﬀerent doping amounts of Sb3+ on the electrochemical
performance of the solid electrolyte were systematically studied. It was found that a small amount of
Sb3+ doping not only increases the concentration of oxygen vacancies, thereby facilitating Li+ ion
transfer, but also induces the segregation of the secondary phase, LiTiPO5, at the grain boundaries,
which promotes grain growth and increases density. Excessive Sb3+ doping, however, shifts the
secondary phase from the orthorhombic to the triclinic system, which is less favorable for Li+ ion
transfer, and leads to abnormal grain growth. The optimal total ionic conductivity of 0.56 mS cm(cid:2)1 was
achieved with a 0.5 wt% addition of Sb2O3, significantly enhancing the bulk density to 2.83 g cm(cid:2)3.
In addition, the Sb3+-doped LATP solid-state electrolyte-assembled full cell provides 152.30 mA h g(cid:2)1
high discharge specific capacity, and can operate stably for 100 cycles, with an average coulomb

1. Introduction

In recent decades, the rapid proliferation of lithium-ion batteries
has revolutionized the vast market for portable electronics, facili-
tated the eﬃcient utilization of clean energy, and mitigated the
escalating environmental crisis and carbon dioxide emissions
resulting from heavy reliance on fossil fuels.1–3 However, conven-
tional
lithium-ion batteries employ liquid electrolytes, which,
despite their high ionic conductivity and excellent electrode sur-
face wettability, pose significant safety risks such as combustion
and explosion due to their inherent thermal instability, poor ion
selectivity, and low electrochemical stability. Consequently, these
traditional lithium-ion batteries fail to meet the stringent safety
requirements and usage conditions.4–6 Therefore, it is imperative
to expedite the development of lithium-ion electrolyte materials
that possess superior safety features, enhanced energy density,
prolonged cycle life, and cost-effectiveness.7,8 Replacing the
liquid electrolyte with a solid electrolyte is a crucial approach
to address these safety concerns.

Yang et al.26 studied the electronic structure of a LLZO solid
electrolyte doped with Sb3+. It is confirmed that Sb3+ can
promote the migration and improvement of lithium ions in
the solid electrolyte at the micro atomic level. Therefore, in this
study, Sb3+ was employed as a dopant with a larger ionic radius
and higher electronegativity to fabricate the LATP–xSb2O3
(x = 0–2.0 wt%) solid electrolyte via the solid phase method.
It was observed that the introduction of Sb3+ effectively sup-
pressed the formation of the secondary phase of the orthor-
hombic system and induced transformation into the triclinic
crystal system, thereby enhancing the concentration of oxygen
vacancies and ultimately improving the ionic conductivity of
the LATP solid electrolyte. The assembled battery showed that
the stability between the electrolyte and lithium metal was
greatly improved, which greatly enhanced its cycle stability.

4. Conclusions

The solid electrolyte material was prepared by doping Sb3+ into
LATP using the solid phase method. The substitution of
unstable Ti4+ with Sb3+, which possesses a larger ionic radius
and higher electronegativity, resulted in enhanced crystal
structure density and increased oxygen vacancy concentration,
thereby improving the ionic conductivity. The total
ionic
conductivity at room temperature was 0.56 mS cm(cid:2)1 and the
activation energy was 0.17 eV after adding 0.5 wt%Sb2O3.
Rietveld refinement revealed that excessive incorporation of
Sb3+ transformed the orthorhombic phase of LiTiPO5 into a
triclinic phase, which adversely aﬀected Li+ ion conduction. It
was further discovered that an appropriate secondary phase
facilitated grain growth and density enhancement, with a
maximum value reaching 2.84 g cm(cid:2)3. In addition, the dis-
charge specific capacity of the assembled full cell can reach
152.30 mA h g(cid:2)1, after 100 cycles, the capacity retention rate is
92.6%, and the average coulomb eﬃciency is 98.5%.
