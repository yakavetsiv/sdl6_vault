High ionic conductivity for solid electrolyte and stable electrode/electrolyte interface have always been the main
requirements for solid-state lithium batteries. Herein, a high ionic conductive composite solid electrolyte (CSE)
film  was  prepared  by  combining  1.3-dioxolanes  (DOL)  capable  of  in-situ  polymerization  with  high  ionic
conductive Li1.5Al0.5Ge1.5(PO4)3 (LAGP)ceramic fillers. The CSE film also shows high interface compatibility and
stability  with  lithium  metal.  The  optimized  poly-DOL  (PDOL)-30%  LAGP  CSE  exhibits  a  desirable  dense  and
(cid:0) 1,  a  high  ion
uniform  structure,  which  endows  the  CSE  with  a  high  ion  conductivity  of  4.63  × 10
migration number up to 0.73 and a wide electrochemical stability window of 5.1 V. The assembled high-voltage
NCM622/PDOL-30%LAGP CSE/Li solid-state cell exhibits good rate and cycle performances with a capacity of
125 mAh/g at 2 C and a capacity retention of 76% at 0.5C after 400 cycles with an average Coulomb efficiency of
99.7%. The PDOL-30%LAGP CSE also possesses a high inhibition ability of lithium dendrites owing to the dense
and uniform structure and the stable electrolyte/lithium electrode interface including Li–Ge, LiF and CF3  com-
ponents. The Li/CSE/Li cell maintains a relatively stable voltage up to 650 h at a current density of 0.1 mA/cm2.
This high ionic conductive composite solid electrolyte film shows a good application prospect in high-voltage
solid-state lithium batteries.

1. Introduction

As the energy utilization is shifting to renewable and low carbon, the
development of efficient energy storage devices is a great way to save
energy [1–3]. The lithium metal can be a strategic material for the next
generation of energy storage devices with small volumes and high en-
ergy(3860  mAh/g)  [4].  But  the  use  of  liquid  organic  electrolytes  in
lithium  batteries  often  poses  safety  risks,  the  non-flammable  solid
electrolytes have been regarded as one of the main alternatives [5,6]. As
the  most  important  component  of  the  solid-state  lithium  battery,  the
high ionic conductivity of solid electrolytes, good interfacial compati-
bility  and  stability,  good  suitability  for  high-voltage  cathode  are  the
basic requirements of solid-state lithium batteries.

At  present,  several  kinds  of  solid  electrolytes  have  been  studied,
including polymer solid electrolytes, eg., polyethylene oxide (PEO) [7],
polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) [8], etc.; inorganic ceramic solid elec-
trolytes materials, eg., sulfide electrolytes [9], pomegranate type solid

Compared with the most widely studied polymers (e.g. PEO, PVDF),
the 1.3-dioxolane (DOL) exists as a liquid at room temperature and can
be polymerized to form solid or gel-state poly-DOL(PDOL) by triggering
polymerization in the presence of LiPF6, which not only can effectively
improve  the  electrochemical  stability  window  of  the  electrolyte  and
avoid  decomposition  and  failure  of  the  electrolyte  [22,23],  but  also
avoid other impurities brought by solvents in the preparation process.

Liu et al. [24] prepared a PDOL gel polymer electrolyte, exhibiting good
electrochemical  properties  and high electrochemical  stability window
(LSV) up  to 4.5V.  Meanwhile,  owing  to the  good interfacial compati-
bility  of  PDOL  with  lithium  anodes  [25],  the  solid  or  gel  PDOL  elec-
trolyte  can  effectively  inhibit  the  growth  of  lithium  dendrites,  which
protects the lithium anode and improves the cycle stability of the cell
[26,27].

In this work, we for the first time envisioned an in-situ compounding
of LAGP and PDOL, making full use of the good interfacial compatibility
of PDOL to solve the poor contact between rigid LAGP and the electrode
and the unstable defect to lithium metal, while the high ionic conduc-
tivity of LAGP can further improve the electrochemical performance of
PDOL. At the same time, the preparation process does not require any
solvents  and  impurities,  the  process  is  simple  and  environmentally
friendly, suitable for scale production. The content of LAGP inorganic
filler  in  the  electrolyte  film  was  adjusted  so  that  the  ion  transport
channels were formed with continuous LAGP particles in the structure.
We also explored the effect of the interaction between PDOL and LAGP
on the electrochemical performance of the composite solid electrolyte
film  and  the  cell  performance.  The  results  showed  that  the  prepared
PDOL/LAGP composite solid electrolyte film has high electrochemical
performance and good interfacial compatibility with lithium electrode,
and endows the high-voltage solid-state lithium battery with stable cycle
performance.

Li–Ge  alloy  is  foud  in  the  SEI  on  the  lithium  sheet  assembled  with
PDOL-30% LAGP as shown in Fig. 5g. According to the previous reports
[39,40], the presence of partial Li–Ge alloy in SEI also facilitates the long
and stable cycling of the cell. Due to the presence of Ge or the Li–Ge
phase in SEI will locally increase the electronic conductivity and will
increase  the  average  transport  coefficient  [41].  It  is  the  reason  why
PDOL-30% LAGP in this paper exhibits better cycling stability and more
excellent  lithium  plating/stripping  capability  than  pristine-PDOL.
Certainly, the proportion of Li–Ge alloys in the SEI film and the mech-
anism of action still need to further analyze experimentally.

lithium  dendrites.  Fig.  6c  shows  the  schematic  diagram  of  the  cell
structure with the composite solid electrolyte. The rigid/flexible struc-
ture of the CSE formed by the polymer and ceramic materials not only
provides continuous ion transport channels for lithium ions from poly-
mer and ceramics respectively, but also provides a flexible interfacial
contact, a high hardness to inhibit the growth of lithium dendrites, thus
reducing  the  concentration  difference  of  ions  and  ensuring  uniform
plating/stripping  of  lithium.  Further,  the  SEI  film  between  the  com-
posite solid electrolyte and lithium anode contains various substances
such as CF3, LiF, and Li–Ge alloys, which all contribute to the interface
stability between electrolyte and lithium anode [45]. These could avoid
the  generation  of  lithium  dendrites  and  thus  facilitate  the  long  and
stable  cycle  of  the  battery.  Therefore,  the  composite  solid  electrolyte
prepared by combining LAGP and PDOL has great potential to be applied
in high-performance solid-state lithium battery.

3. Conclusions

In  summary,  a  high-performance  composite  solid  electrolyte  was
prepared via combining liquid DOL capable of ring-opening polymeri-
zation  under  the  action  of  initiator  with  high  ionic  conductive  LAGP
ceramic  fillers.  The  PDOL-30%  LAGP  CSE  shows  excellent  electro-
(cid:0) 4  S
chemical performance. The ionic conductivity is up to 4.63 × 10
(cid:0) 1, the ion migration number reaches 0.73, and the electrochemical
cm
stability window is 5.1 V, indicating that this CSE can provide excellent
ion transport capability and match high voltage electrodes. These im-
provements are mainly attributed to the dense and uniform structure of
PDOL/LAGP  composite  electrolyte.  The  NCM622/CSE/Li  cell  exhibits

excellent rate and cycle performances with a capacity retention of 76%
after 400 cycles at 0.5C and an average Coulomb efficiency of 99.7%.
The Li/CSE/Li cycling test shows that the PDOL/LAGP combination has
a  positive  effect  on  the  lithium  metal  anode,  which  can  effectively
inhibit the growth of lithium dendrites and provide further assurance for
the stable cycle of the solid-state battery.
