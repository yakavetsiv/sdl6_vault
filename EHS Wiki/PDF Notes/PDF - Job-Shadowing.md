Safety Guidelines and Considerations for Job Shadowing

Job shadowing is increasingly becoming an integral part of the learning experience for students in
elementary and secondary schools. It allows students to observe people as they perform their regular
job duties and to see how skills learned in school are put into action in the workplace. At the University
of Toronto, job shadowing is part of the “U of T Bring Our Children to Work Day” and the “Take Our Kids
to Work Program.” These guidelines have been prepared by the Office of Environmental Health and
Safety to assist the student, parents, workplace sponsor and workplace supervisor to make the
experience a rewarding and safe one. In addition to formal job shadowing activities, these guidelines
are also applicable to the less formal situation where parents bring children under the age of 14 years
into the workplace for any reason.

Legal Requirements

As is applied to the University, the Ontario Occupational Health and Safety Act specifies that no one
under the age of 14 years may be in or about our workplaces unless one of the following conditions
applies:

•  The individual is accompanied at all times by a person who has attained the age of majority, or
•  The individual is in an area of the establishment to which the public generally has access.

Guidelines

In the following guidelines, the term “sponsor” refers to the individual (parent or supervisor) who brings
the student into the workplace and who is responsible for that student during the time spent in the
workplace.

1.  Except in areas of the University to which the public generally has access (e.g., outdoors,
corridors, classrooms and offices), the student must be accompanied at all times by a
responsible adult who is knowledgeable of and trained to deal with any hazards in the work
area.

2.  The  sponsor  must  inform  the  workplace  supervisor  that  the  student  will  be  in  the  workplace
and  agree with  the supervisor on  any activities in which the student  will  take  part taking  into
account the health and safety aspects of these activities.

3.  The sponsor must clearly identify and explain to the student the nature of the hazards in the
workplace and the need for the safety and security procedures applicable to the workplace.
Special attention should be paid to laboratory hazards such as chemical and biological agents,
ionizing radiation and lasers. Entry into the following areas is p rohibited: Level 3 biohazard
laboratories, all construction sites (irrespective of project nature or size) and mechanical,
electrical and other utility rooms.

4.  The  sponsor  must  provide  and  ensure  that  the  student  wears  any  personal  protective
equipment (e.g., safety glasses, laboratory coat, hearing protection, footwear) required in that
workplace.

5.  The student must not operate laboratory or mechanical equipment.

For further information or questions related to these guidelines, contact Environmental Health and
Safety at 416-978-4467 or ehs.office@utoronto.ca.
