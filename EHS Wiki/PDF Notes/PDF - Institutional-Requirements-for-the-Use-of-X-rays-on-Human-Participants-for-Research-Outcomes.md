Institutional Requirements for the Use of X-rays on Human
Participants for Research Outcomes

Background

The  University  of  Toronto  (U  of  T)  conducts  a  wide  range  of  innovative  research  involving  human
participants. This document sets out the requirements for the use of X-rays and X-ray machines to
conduct research involving human research participants at or under the auspices of U of T. These
requirements  take
into  consideration  regulatory  requirements,  ethics  considerations  and
maintaining  radiation  doses  As  Low  As  Reasonably  Achievable  (ALARA).  The  operation  and
registration  of  X-ray  machines  in  Ontario  is  currently  governed  by  the  Healing  Arts  Radiation
Protection (HARP) Act and the Occupational Health & Safety Act (OHSA). This document is issued
pursuant to the University of Toronto Health and Safety Policy and the University of Toronto Policy on
Research Involving Human Subjects to further compliance with those policies and the provisions of
the HARP Act and OHSA.

Regulatory Framework at U of T

The  use  of  X-ray  machines  at  U  of  T  currently  falls  under  two  main  categories:  non-clinical  or
occupational use, and clinical use. Non-clinical systems have a wide range of applications in non-
biological samples and in vivo research with cell cultures or small animals. Clinical X-ray machines
are those used for therapeutic and/or diagnostic treatments in patients as prescribed by a medical
practitioner. This framework provides guidance for the operation of clinical X-ray machines on human
research participants for non-medical purposes.

Non-clinical or occupational use of X-rays

X-ray  machines  that  are  used  for  research  or  non-clinical  purposes  are  regulated  under  the
Occupational  Health  and  Safety  Act  (OHSA),  which  is  enforced  by  the  Ministry  of  Labour,
Immigration, Training & Skills Development (MLITSD). The X-ray safety program developed under this
legislation covers X-ray machines and personnel operating the machines (X-ray workers) at U of T.
The X-ray safety program also applies to U of T workers operating clinical X-ray machines.

The University appoints a competent and qualiﬁed individual as the X-ray Safety Officer (XSO) to act
on its behalf in matters related to the occupational use of X-ray machines with the Ontario MLITSD.
The XSO is a member of the Office of Environmental Health & Safety (EHS) and is responsible for the
implementation and enforcement of the U of T X-ray Safety Program.

Clinical use of X-rays

X-ray  machines  that  are  normally  used  clinically  for  therapeutic  or  diagnostic  purposes  (i.e.,  with
patients) are subject to the HARP Act which is enforced by the Ontario Ministry of Health (MOH). Each
faculty,  department,  or  unit  that  uses  clinical  X-ray  machines  at  U  of  T  is  responsible  for  their
individual clinical X-ray safety program. The requirements under the HARP Act also apply to clinical
X-ray machines used on human research participants.

The  U  of  T  faculty,  department,  or  unit  that  owns  the  clinical  X-ray  machine  is  responsible  for
appointing  a  qualiﬁed  and  competent  Radiation  Protection  Officer  (RPO)  that  meets  the
requirements under Section 9 of the HARP Act. The RPO is responsible for obtaining approval for the
installation and maintenance of clinical X-ray machines. Operators of X-ray machines used for this
purpose must also meet speciﬁc qualiﬁcations under section 5 of the HARP Act. The institutional
XSO is responsible for ensuring that operators of clinical systems are working safely in accordance
with the OHSA.

Institutional Requirements

2.1

Approval by the University

Human Research Ethics Review & Approval

All  researchers  engaging  in  research  activities  involving  human  participants  must  obtain  REB
approval at U of T for their proposed project prior to commencing their work. Researchers wishing to

do so may submit their REB protocol through U of T’s online ethics application system My Research
Human  Protocols  (MRHP).  The  REB  review  ensures  that  the  rights,  safety,  and  welfare  of  human
participants  are  protected.  As  part  of  the  REB’s  review,  they  will  pay  close  attention  to  the
rationale/justiﬁcation of the intervention (i.e., whether exposing participants to ionizing radiation is
ethically acceptable). As part of the researchers’ submission to the REB, they must include:

1.  The proposed effective dose that will be delivered to each human participant per study, and
any  appropriate  modiﬁcations  to  the  dose  based  on  the  unique  characteristics  of  the
participant (e.g., age, weight, sex, etc.);

2.  Inclusion and exclusion criteria for research participants;
3.  The proposed duration of the exposure(s);
4.  The proposed area of exposure of the human participant;
5.  An ethically acceptable justiﬁcation why the study requires exposure of human participants

to ionizing radiation to address the research question;

6.  Discussion on how risks and harms to participants will be minimized and mitigated; and
7.  A plan to manage incidental ﬁndings, if reasonably foreseeable.

EHS Permit Application & Approval

To  maintain  appropriate  institutional  oversight  and  ensure  regulatory  compliance,  clinical  X-ray
machines that are used for research activities involving human participants must be registered under
an EHS X-ray Permit prior to commencing regular operations:

1.  The  Faculty/Department/Unit  should  identify  an  Equipment  Owner  that  has  sufficient

authority to enforce the policies and responsibilities under the permit

2.  The equipment owner is required to apply for an EHS X-ray Permit as the Permit Holder (PH)

for the respective X-ray machine

3.  The equipment owner and permit holder must be tenured or tenure-stream U of T faculty
4.  Prior to commencing the permit application process, the equipment owner must ensure that
the X-ray machine and facility have been registered and approved by the MOH (see Section
2.2).

5.  Permit  applications  with  supporting  documentation  must  be  submitted  to  EHS  Radiation
Protection Service for review and approval granted prior to beginning use of the equipment.

2.2

Approval by the Regulator(s)

U of T Faculties, Departments, or Units referred to as the “owner” of the clinical X-ray machines that
intend on conducting research with human participants must obtain approval from the Ministry of
Health (MOH) by fulﬁlling the following requirements:

1.  Obtain  approval  from  the  MOH  for  the  installation  and  registration  of  the  clinical  X-ray

machine

2.  Submit  X-ray  equipment  and  shielding  speciﬁcations  for  each  machine  located  in  the

designated facility to the MOH

4.  Ensure operators of clinical X-ray machines meet the qualiﬁcations under section 5 of the

2.3

Operation of Clinical X-ray Machines

The “Equipment Owner” of the clinical X-ray machine(s) and the RPO must ensure that clinical X-ray
machines are operated in accordance with all requirements outlined in this framework, as well as
the following provisions under the HARP Act:

a.  No  person  shall  operate  an  X-ray  machine  for  the  irradiation  of  a  human  being  unless  the
person meets the qualiﬁcations and requirements prescribed by Section 5 of the HARP Act.
b.  No  person  shall  operate  an  X-ray  machine  for  the  irradiation  of  a  human  being  unless  the
irradiation has been prescribed by a qualiﬁed medical practitioner or another professional as
prescribed by Section 6 of the HARP Act.

2.4

Personal Radiation Monitoring

Personal radiation monitoring of clinical X-ray operators is regulated by the Ontario MLITSD, Health
Canada Safety Code 35 also sets recommendations for monitoring of personnel. At U of T monitoring
of all personnel operating X-ray machines/sources is the responsibility of the XSO, who implements
the radiation monitoring program through the Office of Environmental Health & Safety. The program
includes quarterly dose monitoring using a federally certiﬁed service provider. The XSO individually
evaluates the risk of exposure to ionizing radiation and recommends a monitoring regime according
to the risk analysis.

2.5

Facility Design & Shielding

It is the responsibility of the U of T Facility RPO to ensure that the design of the X-ray facility that will
house the clinical X-ray machine(s) meets the requirements under Regulation 543 of the HARP Act
and Health Canada Safety Code 35. The RPO may engage the professional advice of the institutional
XSO to assist in the assessment of the need and design for shielding of the X-ray facility.

Roles & Responsibilities

3.1

Faculty/Department/Unit and Equipment Owner

The  Equipment  Owner/Principal  Investigator  of  a  clinical  X-ray  machine  has  the  following
responsibilities within the institutional framework:

1.  Submitting  research  proposals  involving  human  research  participants  for  REB  review  and

approval

2.  Applying  for  and  obtaining  approval  from  the  MOH  in  accordance  with  Section  2.2,  with

support from EHS upon request.

3.  Applying for and maintaining an EHS X-ray Safety Permit for the X-ray machine and facility
4.  Participating in U of T EHS X-ray Safety program and annual EHS safety inspection program
5.  Following and complying with the regulations under the Ontario HARP Act and requirements

by the Ontario Ministry of Health

6.  Ensuring qualiﬁed X-ray machine operators and facility occupants participate in EHS X-ray

Safety training as well as providing job-speciﬁc training
7.  Appointing a qualiﬁed Radiation Protection Officer (RPO)
8.  Consenting research participants and ensuring they are made aware of the risks associated

with exposure to ionizing radiation in addition to other study-related risks

3.2

Facility Radiation Protection Officer (RPO)

The Facility RPO is to be appointed by the responsible Faculty, Department, Unit or Equipment Owner
of the clinical X-ray machine and approved by the X-ray Inspection Service (XRIS) of the MOH. The
responsibilities of the RPO include but are not limited to:

1.  Registering and maintaining registration of all applicable clinical X-ray machines and X-ray

facilities with the Ontario Ministry of Health.

2.  Establishing and maintaining procedures and quality tests for the safe operation of the X-ray

equipment as per R.R.O. 1990, Regulation 543.

3.  Ensuring that the X-ray equipment is maintained and meets the standards prescribed by the

regulations as per R.R.O. 1990, Regulation 543.

4.  Ensure acceptance and quality testing of the clinical X-ray machines is performed according

to R.R.O. 1990, Regulation 543.

5.  Ensuring that all X-ray operators are qualiﬁed according to the HARP Act and Health Canada

Code 35 requirements.

6.  Ensuring  operations  of  an  approved  clinical  X-ray  machine  and  X-ray  facility  are  in
compliance with all regulations and requirements under the HARP Act,  R.S.O. 1990, c. H.2.

3.3

Environmental Health & Safety (EHS)

The Office of Environmental Health & Safety and the institutional X-ray Safety Officer (XSO) have the
following responsibilities:

4.  Providing research ethics training and education for researchers
